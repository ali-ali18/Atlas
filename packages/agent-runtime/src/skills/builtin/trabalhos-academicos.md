# Academic work (ABNT)

Goal: work the student can hand in and defend — correct structure, honest sources, citations that match the references, and a file formatted to the norms. You help them think and write; you never invent data, quotes or references.

## 1. Start by fixing the frame

Before writing, find out (ask only what is missing, in one short message):

- **Type of work** and its norm:
  - research project → NBR 15287;
  - TCC, monograph, dissertation or thesis → NBR 14724;
  - scientific article → NBR 6022;
  - technical or scientific report → NBR 10719.
- **Course, discipline and level** (graduação, especialização, mestrado), and the **theme** or question.
- **Institution manual.** Most universities publish their own manual, which details or overrides the generic norm (font, cover layout, logo, margins for printing). If the user has it, follow it over these defaults. Ask for it or search for it ("manual de normalização" + university name).
- **Deadline, expected length, and what already exists** (draft, advisor comments, required bibliography).

## 2. Structure

**Research project (NBR 15287).** Elements, in order:
1. capa and folha de rosto;
2. tema and delimitação;
3. problema de pesquisa;
4. hipótese(s), when applicable;
5. objetivos (geral and específicos, written with verbs in the infinitive);
6. justificativa;
7. referencial teórico;
8. metodologia (type of research, approach, population or sample, instruments, data analysis, ethics when people are involved);
9. recursos;
10. cronograma;
11. referências.

**Academic work (NBR 14724).**
- **Pre-textual elements.**
  - Required: capa, folha de rosto, resumo in Portuguese, resumo in a foreign language, sumário.
  - Optional or institution-dependent: errata, folha de aprovação, dedicatória, agradecimentos, epígrafe, listas de ilustrações, tabelas, abreviaturas and símbolos.
- **Textual part:** introdução, desenvolvimento, conclusão.
- **Post-textual elements.**
  - Required: referências.
  - Optional: glossário, apêndices (made by the author), anexos (by others), índice.

**Abstract (NBR 6028).**
- One paragraph that gives the objective, method, results and conclusion.
- In academic work it usually has 150 to 500 words.
- Follow it with "Palavras-chave:" — 3 to 5 terms separated by semicolons and ending with a period.

**Section numbering (NBR 6024).**
- Use progressive Arabic numbering: 1, 1.1, 1.1.1.
- Primary sections start on a new page.
- Make title levels visually distinct, the same in the text and in the sumário (NBR 6027).

## 3. Research with reliable sources

Follow the built-in skill "Pesquisar". For academic work, add these rules:

**Prefer scholarly and primary sources:**
- Peer-reviewed articles, theses and dissertations.
- Books from academic publishers.
- Official statistics, laws, and technical norms.

**Good places to search:**
- Google Scholar, SciELO, Portal de Periódicos CAPES, BDTD (Brazilian theses and dissertations), and university repositories.
- PubMed for health, ERIC for education, arXiv for preprints (say they are preprints).
- Brazilian official data:
  - general: IBGE (SIDRA), IPEA, the Banco Central, INEP;
  - health and labour: DATASUS, the Ministério da Saúde, CAGED and RAIS;
  - legislation: planalto.gov.br;
  - other: the official site of the agency or body that publishes the figure.

**Check every source before using it:**
- Who wrote it, where it was published (journal, publisher, agency), the year, and the DOI or stable URL.
- Open the source itself; never cite from a search snippet or from another author's summary when the original is reachable.
- For statistics, cite the original survey or table with its reference period, not a news article about it.

**Never invent anything.**
- No author, title, year, page, DOI or quote.
- If you can't confirm a reference, leave it out and tell the user, or mark it clearly as "não confirmado".
- A fabricated reference can fail the student. Not having one is far better.

**Quality signals:**
- Prefer recent work (roughly the last 5–10 years) unless the topic needs classic authors.
- Prefer sources that are cited by other scholarly work.
- Say plainly when evidence is weak or contradictory.
- Avoid Wikipedia, blogs, content farms, AI-generated pages and sites that sell essays as sources. Wikipedia may help you find primary sources, never as a citation.

**Keep a source log** while researching: full reference data, the URL or DOI, the access date for online sources, and the exact page of each quote. References are built from this log.

## 4. Citations (NBR 10520, 2023 version)

**Author-date system.**
- In the current version the author's surname is written with only the first letter capitalized, inside and outside parentheses: "Segundo Silva (2020, p. 15)…" or "(Silva, 2020, p. 15)".
- Some institutions still require the older uppercase form ("(SILVA, 2020)"). Follow their manual if it says so.

**Direct quotation** (the exact words) always carries the page (or the location for unpaged sources).
- Up to 3 lines: inside the paragraph, in double quotes.
- More than 3 lines:
  - its own block, indented 4 cm from the left margin;
  - smaller font (usually 10 pt) and single spacing;
  - no quotes.

**Other forms.**
- Indirect quotation (paraphrase): the page is optional.
- Citation of a citation: "Freire (1987 apud Silva, 2020, p. 10)". Use it only when the original is truly unavailable.

**Multiple authors.**
- With 1 to 3 authors, name all of them.
- With 4 or more, you may write the first author followed by "et al.", consistently.

**Every citation must match exactly one entry in the references, and every reference must be cited.**

## 5. References (NBR 6023)

- Put them in a list at the end, in alphabetical order. Align them to the left, single-spaced, with one blank line between entries.
- Highlight the title the same way throughout (bold is the most common).
- Common patterns:
  - Book: SOBRENOME, Prenome. **Título**: subtítulo. Edição. Local: Editora, ano.
  - Article: SOBRENOME, Prenome. Título do artigo. **Nome da Revista**, Local, v. X, n. Y, p. início-fim, ano. DOI: ….
  - Thesis or dissertation: SOBRENOME, Prenome. **Título**. Ano. Tipo (Grau em Área) – Instituição, Local, ano.
  - Online document: … Disponível em: URL. Acesso em: 14 out. 2026.
  - Law: BRASIL. Lei nº …, de … . Ementa. **Diário Oficial da União**: … . Disponível em: … . Acesso em: … .
- In the reference list the author's surname stays in uppercase (SILVA, João).
- Include the DOI whenever one exists.

## 6. Writing

- Write in an academic register:
  - impersonal or first person plural, as the course requires;
  - precise and without hype;
  - one idea per paragraph.
- Connect each section to the research problem and objectives. The conclusion answers the objectives; it adds no new data.
- Write *with* the student, not for them:
  - draft, explain choices, and suggest what they should read and verify;
  - respect their institution's rules on AI use;
  - when asked, show which parts came from you so they can disclose it.
- Plagiarism: paraphrase genuinely and cite. Never "rewrite to avoid the plagiarism detector" a text that is not theirs.

## 7. The file

For short or simple documents, use the quick path below. For a full academic deliverable (TCC, monograph, project with cover, abstract, table of contents, figures and tables), use the production path in section 8.

Quick path: use `create_file` with `layout: "abnt"` for `.docx` (preferred, since students edit and their advisor comments) and `.pdf`. Send the whole work as one Markdown `content` (in parts if it is long: `layout` goes on the first part). The layout is applied by the server; write plain Markdown plus the few markers below.

**What the ABNT layout applies (NBR 14724):**
- A4 paper, margins 3 cm top/left and 2 cm bottom/right.
- Times New Roman 12 pt, 1.5 line spacing, justified paragraphs, first-line indent of 1.25 cm, no extra space between paragraphs.
- `#` primary section: bold uppercase, 12 pt, starts a new page. Numbered titles ("1 INTRODUÇÃO") are left-aligned; unnumbered ones (RESUMO, ABSTRACT, SUMÁRIO, AGRADECIMENTOS, REFERÊNCIAS, GLOSSÁRIO, APÊNDICE A, ANEXO A…) are centered.
- `##` secondary: bold. `###` tertiary: bold italic. `####` and deeper: italic.
- Block quote (`>`): indented 4 cm, 10 pt, single-spaced, justified.
- Lines starting with "Fonte:", "Nota:" and captions such as "Tabela 1 – …" or "Figura 2 – …": 10 pt, single-spaced, left-aligned, no indent. Tables are 10 pt with thin borders.
- Everything before the textual part (resumo, abstract, sumário…) is single-spaced with no indent, as NBR 6028 asks.
- Under a `# REFERÊNCIAS` heading, each paragraph or list item is one entry: single-spaced, left-aligned, no indent, one blank line between entries.
- Page numbers: top right, 10 pt, counted from the folha de rosto (the capa is not counted) and shown only from the first page of the textual part.

**Markdown conventions.** Every marker goes alone on its line. All are optional: without them you still get a correct ABNT body, just without capa and folha de rosto.

- **Capa**: `<!-- capa -->` … `<!-- /capa -->`. Inside, separate three zones with a line of `---`: top (institution, course, author), middle (`# Title` and subtitle or type of work) and bottom (city, then year). Put each line in its own paragraph (blank line between them). Everything is centered.
- **Folha de rosto**: `<!-- folha-de-rosto -->` … `<!-- /folha-de-rosto -->`, same three zones: top (author), middle (`# Title`, then the nature of the work as a `>` quote, which this block indents 8 cm, then "Orientador: …"), bottom (city, year).
- **Pre-textual elements**: write them after the folha de rosto as `#` sections, in order (RESUMO, ABSTRACT, lists…). Each `#` opens its own page. For an element without a title (dedicatória, epígrafe) put `<!-- pagebreak -->` before it.
- **Textual part**: starts at the first `#` heading whose title begins with a number ("1 INTRODUÇÃO") or is "Introdução". To start it elsewhere, put `<!-- textual -->` right before it. Page numbers show from there.
- **Page break**: `<!-- pagebreak -->` (or `\pagebreak`). You rarely need it; `#` sections already start pages.
- **Sumário**: write only `<!-- sumario -->` where it goes (after the abstract, before the introdução). Do not write its entries: the server builds it from the `#`, `##`, `###` and `####` titles of the textual and post-textual parts (including REFERÊNCIAS, APÊNDICE and ANEXO), with the same emphasis as the titles, dotted leaders and the page numbers. A `# SUMÁRIO` heading alone does the same, and anything written under it is replaced. So the titles you write are exactly what appears in it.

**Skeleton.**

```
<!-- capa -->
UNIVERSIDADE …

CURSO DE …

Author Name

---

# Title of the work

Subtitle or type of work

---

City

Year
<!-- /capa -->

<!-- folha-de-rosto -->
Author Name

---

# Title of the work

> Nature of the work, institution and purpose.

Orientador: Prof. Dr. …

---

City

Year
<!-- /folha-de-rosto -->

# RESUMO
…
# ABSTRACT
…
<!-- sumario -->

# 1 INTRODUÇÃO
…
## 1.1 Problema de pesquisa
…
# REFERÊNCIAS
…
```

**What to promise the student.**
- Page numbers and the sumário need no manual work in the `.pdf`: the numbers are measured on the real layout.
- In the `.docx` the page numbers are computed by Word itself, and the sumário is a real Word table of contents, pre-filled with the page numbers measured on the PDF layout of the same text. Word may ask to update fields when opening it: answering yes refreshes it with Word's own pagination, which can differ slightly from the PDF. Do not tell the student to fix numbering by hand.
- Not generated: lista de ilustrações, de tabelas, de abreviaturas, glossário and índice. If the work needs them, write them as plain `#` sections and say that their page numbers are not filled in.
- The font is Times New Roman. If the institution manual asks for something different (Arial, a logo on the cover), say what you could not apply automatically so the student can adjust it in Word.

**Before delivering, check:**
- every citation is in the references and vice versa;
- the numbering is consistent with the sumário;
- no reference was left "não confirmado" without telling the user.

## 8. Production path (full deliverables)

Prefer `run_code`, which has pandoc, LibreOffice, poppler, python-docx and LaTeX with abnTeX2 installed, plus templates and a helper module. Work in this order:

1. **Write the content** in Markdown (for Word) or LaTeX (for PDF), with real references. Save the user's attachments with `files` (read from `in/<name>`).
2. **Build the file with the shipped templates.** In the script: `import sys; sys.path.insert(0, "/app"); import doclib`.
   - Word (`.docx`, the format the student and advisor edit): `doclib.md_to_docx_abnt("in/work.md", "/work/out/work.docx", toc=True)`. It uses `/app/templates/abnt/abnt-reference.docx` (A4, margins 3/2 cm, Times New Roman 12, 1.5 spacing, 1.25 cm indent, justified, ABNT heading styles, long quote, caption, source and reference styles, page number at top right). Use pandoc blocks for special styles: `::: {custom-style="Fonte"}` for "Fonte:" lines and `::: {custom-style="Referencias"}` for the reference list. `doclib.docx_add_toc(path)` adds a table of contents field to an existing `.docx`.
   - PDF with exact ABNT typography (abnTeX2, offline): `doclib.fill_template("abnt-trabalho", "/work/work.tex", TITULO=..., AUTOR=..., TEXTO=...)` (or `abnt-projeto` for a research project), a `referencias.bib` next to it, then `doclib.latex_abnt("/work/work.tex")`. Citations use `\cite{key}` and `\citeonline{key}` (author-date).
3. **Convert to PDF and look at every page.** `pdf = doclib.docx_to_pdf(path, outdir="/work")` (it also refreshes the table of contents), then `doclib.pdf_to_pngs(pdf, dpi=60)` writes the pages to `/work/preview/`, and they come back to you as images (12 per call; use `first=` to continue, `dpi=90` to read small text). They are not delivered to the user. Keep conversion output out of `/work/out/`.
4. **Check against this list** and fix the source and rebuild until everything passes:
   - capa: centered zones, nothing cut off, no page number;
   - folha de rosto: author, title, nature of the work indented 8 cm, advisor, city and year;
   - resumo and abstract: single-spaced, one paragraph, keywords line;
   - sumário: titles match the document, dotted leaders, page numbers filled and correct;
   - headings: primary in bold uppercase, secondary bold, tertiary bold italic, numbered consistently, no heading alone at the bottom of a page;
   - body: justified, 1.5 spacing, 1.25 cm first-line indent, no stray blank paragraphs;
   - long quotes: 4 cm indent, 10 pt, single-spaced, no quotation marks;
   - tables: IBGE style (horizontal rules only, no side borders), not cut off the page; title above and "Fonte:" below, 10 pt;
   - figures: title above, "Fonte:" below, readable at print size;
   - page numbers: top right, with the right values (cover not counted, shown from the first textual page);
   - references: alphabetical, single-spaced, left-aligned, blank line between entries, each cited in the text and vice versa.
5. **Deliver** the final `.docx` (and the `.pdf` if the user asked or the institution requires it) saved in `/work/out/`. Tell the student what is not automatic (for example, opening the `.docx` in Word may ask to update fields; lists of figures or tables are not generated).

If your model cannot view images, say that the layout was not checked visually and verify what you can with text (page count and extracted text per page).
